The AI Revolution Through the Lens of History

The AI “bubble” everyone fears? A book from 2002 already told us how it ends — two bubbles, not one, and the winners show up only after the burst.

Xinshuai Qi, Ph.D. July 2026 After Carlota Perez, Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital (2002)

In 2002, economist Carlota Perez published Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital. After studying five great surges — canals, railways, steel, electricity, and the internet — she found they don’t repeat, but they rhyme. And the rhyme is precise enough to be useful.

Here is the pattern, and where AI sits inside it today.

1 · Every revolution has two bubbles, not one

This is the part most people miss. A technology revolution doesn’t inflate once — it inflates twice.

Bubble one · Infrastructure

Lay the rails. String the grid. Bury the fiber. Stack the GPUs. Investment is enormous, the logic is sound, and the returns arrive late — capital pours in years before cash flows out.

Bubble two · Application

Only after the technology matures and the cost collapses do the companies that actually make money show up.

Every surge leaves a physical inheritance. Railway mania left a rail network. The dot-com mania left fiber and, eventually, cloud computing. The AI mania will leave behind a global compute-and-power grid. We are still inside bubble one — the winners of bubble two mostly don’t exist yet, or are too small to notice.

2 · A burst is not a failure

Railways crashed. Electricity crashed. The internet crashed in 2000. Every one of them went on to become the invisible infrastructure the modern world runs on.

The crash is not “the technology failed.” It is “capital ran faster than reality could follow.” The burst does one useful thing: it clears the excess hardware. And precisely because there is excess, the price of infrastructure collapses — which is what finally lights the fuse on the application layer. Cheap fiber built AWS. Cheap compute will build whatever comes next. The overshoot is not a bug in the cycle. It is the mechanism.

3 · The Hype Cycle is real — and AI is at a known point on it

Gartner’s curve — Innovation Trigger → Peak of Inflated Expectations → Trough of Disillusionment → Slope of Enlightenment → Plateau of Productivity — is not marketing. The internet followed it exactly. AI is following it now.

My read: AI is late in the Peak of Inflated Expectations, beginning the handoff toward the Trough. We are near the profit peak of the infrastructure build, and 2–3 years from the shakeout.

4 · The shovel-sellers move first

In every Kondratiev recovery, the first thing to re-rate is the “sell the shovels” layer.

  • Railway era: rails and heavy industry.
  • Electricity era: generation equipment.
  • Internet era: fiber, routers, switches.
  • AI era: GPUs, servers, data centers — and, increasingly, electricity itself.

That is exactly what the market has priced first. This is normal. But it marks where we are in the cycle, not where the durable value will finally sit.

5 · The timeline: an 18–20 year super-cycle

Industry-cycle research (including work from firms like BlackRock) frames the AI super-cycle as roughly 18–20 years, 2023–2042, in four acts:

2023–2028Infrastructure frenzy.Compute, foundation models, data centers. Money first, discipline later.
2028–2033Application landing + shakeout.Overbuilt capacity, price wars, most “AI companies” die — while the applications that genuinely work start to earn.
2033–2038Commercial boom.AI saturates industry after industry.
2038–2042Platform lock-in.The structure sets; a few giants consolidate.

Multiple sources point to 2027–2029 as the burst window. Plausible markers: leaders like Nvidia and OpenAI seeing valuations compress on the order of ~70%; the Nasdaq-100 correcting 30–40% (not the ~78% of 2000); 70–80% of AI startups folding.

6 · This is not 2000 — and the difference matters

  • 2000 was a story bubble: zero revenue, infinite narrative. 2026 is a valuation bubble: real revenue, over-heated multiples.
  • In 2000, the busted companies went to zero. In 2026, a correction hits Microsoft, Google, Nvidia — businesses that keep earning even as multiples compress. The equity gets cheaper; the cash flow doesn’t vanish.
  • AI carries national-credit backing. The US and China both treat it as strategic priority — an institutional moat the dot-coms never had.

So the bet worth making is not “the crash comes in year X.” It is the pattern itself: over-investment → cost collapse → application explosion → business-model reconstruction. If AI runs that script, the biggest winner won’t be whoever owns the smartest model. It will be whoever embeds AI deepest into an industry’s workflow, compounds a data flywheel, and builds system-level engineering capability.

7 · What the market will actually reward after the burst

The trough is a brutal, efficient sorting machine. In the frenzy, everyone can raise on a story. After the burst, financial capital exits and production capital takes over — and the question flips from “how big could this get?” to “how much does it save or earn right now?” Four hard tests separate survivors from the culled:

Test 1 · Takes over a cost center

Nice-to-have “enhancement tools” (the 10%-faster copilots) get cut first in a downturn. Survivors sell labor-as-a-service: they enter the P&L directly, cutting a real cost (support, basic code, first-pass legal review) by 80%+, or creating a new revenue line.

Test 2 · Embedded in the workflow

“Technically ahead” is a fragile moat — models commoditize fast. If a user copies text in and out, they’ll switch tomorrow. If the tool has fused your ERP, CRM, and knowledge base and rebuilt the process around itself, replacing it means an organizational rebuild.

Test 3 · Unit economics close

In the frenzy, nobody counts the compute. After the burst, unit economics must be positive — no more spending $1 of compute to produce $0.10 of output. As chips advance and inference moves on-device / to small models, revenue has to cover depreciation and marginal inference.

Test 4 · Owns exclusive context

Survivors are not “wrappers on a big model.” They are containers for proprietary industry data and workflow. The model supplies general intelligence; solving the hard problem takes decades of non-public data, compliance rules, and expert judgment. Model + proprietary context is the moat.

8 · History already ran this test

Revolution The frenzy’s “fake” apps What survived & won
Steam & rail (1840s)Speculative “tourist lines” with no freightTrunk networks connecting mines, ports, industrial towns — cut society-wide logistics cost
Electricity & mass production (1920s)Factories that just swapped a steam engine for a motorThose that redesigned around the assembly line (Ford) — a step-change in yield
Internet (2000s)Pets.com — no fulfillment, pure eyeballsAmazon (commerce + AWS), Google (precision search ads) once infra got cheap
[Internet 2000] → clear excess fiber/servers → Amazon / Google
[Mobile 2011] → clear the clone-app frenzy → mobile payments / Uber / ByteDance
[AI 2027+] → clear compute glut + wrapper-SaaS → vertical Agents / embodied AI / AI-driven drug & engineering

9 · So where do the 2030s winners come from?

If 2027–2029 clears the AI valuation excess and infrastructure prices crater, three application shapes look most likely to open the next golden age:

AI architecture-rebuilders in high-risk verticals

Legal/compliance first-pass, preclinical molecule screening, complex financial analysis, chip EDA assist. The cost of an hour is extreme; AI doesn’t need to replace the human 100% — compressing a 100-hour expert task to 2 hours (human-in-the-loop) already commands a premium.

Physical-world operators (Physical AI)

Industrial robotics, unmanned operations in hard environments, autonomous logistics. Pure-software efficiency has a ceiling. Turning compute into physical output — replacing expensive, scarce skilled labor — is the best outlet for the compute glut and the cleanest path to enormous ROI.

Enterprise Agent orchestration

Cross-system ERP/CRM automation: supply-chain replenishment, invoice reconciliation, claims processing, run end-to-end. The shift from Chat to Action — the indispensable “digital-employee layer” inside a company’s operations.

10 · What this means — including for you

For builders and operators, the uncomfortable truth stays the same: the winning skill isn’t building bigger models. It’s the boring work — data governance, evaluation, monitoring, integration. The moat is engineering discipline, not model IQ.

For everyone else, the historical playbook is unglamorous but reliable:

  • Don’t chase the top. When the people around you who never touch stocks start trading AI names, that’s the most dangerous signal.
  • Hold cash for the low. After the burst the market grinds sideways at the bottom — the best entry into the next big cycle.
  • Bet on boring engineering capability. The survivors are the ones who made data governance, evaluation, and operations genuinely solid.

History doesn’t repeat, but it rhymes closely. The AI revolution’s success will come with a burst — and the burst won’t kill the technology. It only kills inflated valuations and fragile business models. AI is mid-infrastructure, high in the cycle. The profit peak is 2–3 years out; the shakeout isn’t far (2027–2029). The real opportunity isn’t chasing the peak — it’s the companies with real use cases, real cash flow, and real engineering that surface after the burst.

Which layer are you betting on — the model, or the application?

从历史的镜子看 AI 革命

所有人都在担心的 AI「泡沫」？一本 2002 年的书早就写好了结局 — 两轮泡沫，而非一轮，赢家只在破裂之后才出现。

祁新帅 (Xinshuai Qi), Ph.D. 2026 年 7 月 基于卡洛塔·佩雷斯《技术革命与金融资本》(2002)

2002 年，经济学家卡洛塔·佩雷斯（Carlota Perez）出版了《技术革命与金融资本》。她研究了运河、铁路、钢铁、电力和互联网这五次大浪潮，发现历史不会简单重复，但会高度押韵 — 而且押得足够精确，可以拿来用。

下面是这套规律，以及 AI 今天所处的位置。

一 · 每次革命都有两轮泡沫，不是一轮

这是大多数人忽略的地方。一次技术革命不是膨胀一次，而是膨胀两次。

第一轮 · 基础设施

修铁路、建电网、铺光纤、堆算力。投资巨大、逻辑成立、回报滞后 — 资本在现金流出来之前很多年就涌了进去。

第二轮 · 应用爆发

只有当技术成熟、成本下降之后，真正赚钱的公司才会出现。

每一轮浪潮都留下实体遗产：铁路泡沫留下铁路网，互联网泡沫留下光纤和云计算，AI 泡沫将沉淀一张全球算力与电力的网络。我们现在还在第一轮里 — 第二轮的赢家，大多还不存在，或者小到看不见。

二 · 泡沫破裂 ≠ 技术失败

铁路崩过，电力崩过，互联网 2000 年也崩过。但它们最后都成了现代世界赖以运转的隐形基础设施。

破裂的本质不是「技术失败」，而是「资本跑得太快，现实跟不上」。破裂只做一件有用的事：清理过度的硬件冗余。而正因为有冗余，基础设施价格才会大幅下降 — 这才最终点燃了应用层的爆发。便宜的光纤造就了 AWS，便宜的算力会造就下一个东西。过度投资不是周期里的 bug，它就是那个机制本身。

三 · 技术成熟度曲线是真实的 — 而 AI 正处在一个已知点上

Gartner 的曲线 — 技术萌芽 → 期望膨胀 → 泡沫破裂低谷 → 稳步爬升 → 生产力平稳 — 不是营销话术。互联网严格地走过了这条曲线，AI 正在走。

我的判断：AI 正处在「期望膨胀期」的后段，开始向「破裂低谷」交棒。我们已接近基建投资的利润顶，离出清还有 2–3 年。

四 ·「卖铲子」的先涨

每一轮康波周期的复苏期，最先被重新定价的都是「卖铲子」这一环。

  • 铁路时代：铁轨和重工业。
  • 电力时代：发电设备。
  • 互联网时代：光缆、路由器、交换机。
  • AI 时代：GPU、服务器、数据中心，以及越来越关键的 — 电力本身。

这正是市场率先定价的对象。这很正常。但它标记的是我们在周期里的位置，而不是长期价值最终会沉淀的地方。

五 · 时间表：一个 18–20 年的超级周期

产业周期研究（包括贝莱德等机构的工作）把 AI 超级周期框定为大约 18–20 年，2023–2042，分四幕：

2023–2028基建狂热期。算力、大模型、数据中心疯狂投资。先砸钱，后讲纪律。
2028–2033应用落地 + 泡沫出清期。基建过剩、价格战、大量「AI 公司」倒闭 — 而真正能用的应用开始赚钱。
2033–2038商业繁荣期。AI 全面渗透千行百业。
2038–2042平台固化期。格局稳定，少数巨头垄断。

多个来源指向 2027–2029 是破裂的关键窗口。可能的标志性事件：英伟达、OpenAI 等估值下跌约 70%；纳斯达克 100 回调 30–40%（而非 2000 年那种约 78% 的崩盘）；70–80% 的 AI 初创企业倒闭。

六 · 这不是 2000 年 — 而差异至关重要

  • 2000 年是纯故事泡沫：零收入 + 无限故事。2026 年是估值过热泡沫：真实收入 + 过高估值。
  • 2000 年破产的公司会归零；2026 年若回调，受影响的是微软、谷歌、英伟达 — 这些公司作为生意仍在赚钱，只是估值倍数被压缩。股票变便宜了，现金流并没有消失。
  • AI 有国家信用支撑。中美都把 AI 视为战略优先级 — 这是互联网公司当年没有的「制度护城河」。

所以真正值得相信的，不是「某一年一定崩」，而是这套演进路径本身：基础设施过度投资 → 成本快速下降 → 应用爆发 → 商业模式重构。如果 AI 遵循这一规律，最大的赢家很可能不是拥有最强模型的公司，而是能把 AI 最深地嵌入行业工作流、持续积累数据飞轮、并构建系统级工程能力的企业。

七 · 泡沫破裂后，市场真正会奖励什么

低谷是一台极其残酷、也极其高效的分拣机。狂热期人人都能靠故事融资；破裂后金融资本退场，生产资本接管 — 核心问题从「未来能变多大」切换为「眼下能省多少、赚多少」。四道硬标准，把幸存者和被淘汰者分开：

标准一 · 接管成本中心

锦上添花的「增强型工具」（帮你快 10% 的副驾驶）在紧缩期会被第一个砍掉。幸存者卖的是劳动结果即服务：直接进入损益表，把一项真实成本（客服、基础代码、法律初审）降低 80% 以上，或直接创造全新收入流。

标准二 · 深度嵌入工作流

「技术领先」是极脆弱的壁垒 — 模型会快速商品化。用户复制粘贴进去、用完即走，明天就能换掉你；而如果它打通了 ERP、CRM、知识库并围绕自己重构了流程，替换它就意味着一次组织重构。

标准三 · 单位经济自洽

狂热期没人算算力；破裂后单位经济必须为正 — 不能再用 1 美元算力去生成 0.1 美元的产出。随着芯片演进、推理走向端侧 / 小模型，变现收入必须覆盖折旧与边际推理成本。

标准四 · 掌控独家上下文

幸存者几乎都不是「大模型的壳」，而是「行业专有数据与工作流的容器」。大模型提供通用智力，真正解决难题靠的是行业沉淀数十年的非公开数据、合规规范和专家经验。模型 + 专有 Context，才是护城河。

八 · 历史已经跑过这场测试

革命周期 狂热期的「伪应用」 存活并爆发的真应用
蒸汽与铁路 (1840s)投机性、无货源的「观光线路」连通煤矿、港口、工业重镇的干线货运网 — 降低全社会物流成本
电力与批量生产 (1920s)只是把蒸汽机换成电机的旧工厂围绕流水线重新设计的（福特）— 效率与良率的阶梯式飞跃
互联网 (2000s)Pets.com — 无履约能力、纯眼球经济亚马逊（电商 + AWS）、谷歌（精准搜索广告）在基建降价后胜出
[互联网 2000] → 出清过剩光纤/服务器 → 亚马逊 / 谷歌
[移动互联网 2011] → 出清山寨应用狂热 → 移动支付 / Uber / 字节
[AI 革命 2027+] → 出清算力过剩 + 套壳 SaaS → 垂直 Agent / 具身智能 / AI 驱动药物与工程

九 · 那么 2030 年代的赢家从哪来？

如果 2027–2029 出清 AI 估值泡沫、基础设施价格暴跌，以下三类应用形态最有可能开启下一个黄金时代：

高风险垂直行业的「AI 架构重构者」

法律合规初审、临床前分子筛选、复杂金融分析、芯片 EDA 辅助。这些行业单位时间成本极高；AI 不需要 100% 替代人类 — 只要把 100 小时的高薪专家工作压缩到 2 小时（Human-in-the-loop），就能创造极高溢价。

物理世界接管者（Physical AI）

工业自动化机器人、特种场景无人化运维、自动驾驶物流。纯软件的增效有天花板；把算力转化为物理世界的生产力、替代昂贵且短缺的技术劳动力，是消化过剩算力、实现巨额 ROI 的最佳出口。

企业级智能体流转系统

跨系统的 ERP / CRM 自动化：供应链补货、发票对账、客户理赔，端到端跑完。从「对话（Chat）」演进到「执行（Action）」— 成为企业运营中不可或缺的「数字员工层」。

十 · 这意味着什么 — 也包括对你

对建造者和经营者，那个不太舒服的真相始终没变：真正的护城河不是更大的模型，而是那些「无聊」的工程 — 数据治理、评估体系、运维监控、系统集成。护城河是工程纪律，不是模型智商。

对其他所有人，历史的剧本并不炫目，但可靠：

  • 不要追高。当你身边从不碰股票的人都开始聊 AI 概念股时，那是最危险的信号。
  • 留现金等低点。破裂后市场会在底部低位震荡 — 那是布局下一个大周期的最好窗口。
  • 押注「无聊的工程能力」。能穿越泡沫的公司，是把数据治理、评估、运维真正做扎实的那一批。

历史不会简单重复，但韵脚高度相似。AI 革命的成功必然伴随泡沫破裂 — 而破裂不会杀死技术，只会杀死高估的估值和脆弱的商业模式。当前 AI 处于基建期中段、周期高位。离利润顶还有 2–3 年，离出清（2027–2029）已经不远。真正的机会不在泡沫顶峰追高，而在破裂之后 — 那些有真实场景、现金流和工程能力的公司会浮出水面。
