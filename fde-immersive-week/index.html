From Waterfall to Venn Diagrams: What a Week of Immersive FDE Work Taught Me

Ten projects. Sixteen pods. One week inside a new model of delivery — where disciplines overlap, everyone builds, and human judgment becomes more valuable precisely because AI can do more.

Xinshuai Qi, Ph.D. September 2026 8-minute read

Context

It was a full week — 10 projects, 16 pods running in parallel. A few weeks ago, I took part in an immersive Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) project experience at Deloitte University (DU). I wanted to organize my thoughts and share what I took away from it.

What Has to Change: From Waterfall to Venn Diagram

The development model is shifting from Waterfall to something closer to a Venn diagram. Product Design, Architecture, Data, AI, Test, DevOps, and CI/CD are no longer a relay race of sequential handoffs. They overlap, and they happen at the same time, in the same room.

  • Everyone needs to stretch the horizontal bar of their T-shaped skill set — the breadth, not just the depth.
  • Building your own knowledge tree — your own knowledge system — matters more now than it ever has. The minutiae of execution can be handed off to AI. The people who survive in this new team model are the ones with big-picture thinking, cross-disciplinary range, strong communication, sharp judgment, and good questions. People with a narrow, single-track knowledge base will struggle to keep pace — they are behind before the project even starts, and just keeping up will already feel like an achievement. A real, substantial contribution becomes rare, or very limited.

The way teams actually operate is shifting too:

  • When every team member is moving at full speed, how you handle coordination and documentation becomes critical — and the community is still figuring out best practices. This is exactly why every project still needs a lead or manager; the “middle management is dead” theory does not hold up. But flat structures, small teams, and decentralized organizations are absolutely where things are heading.
  • People who cannot adapt to this way of working will get phased out. Given a choice between a 10x employee and a 1x employee, no company is choosing the 1x employee.
  • Every team member needs to get good at switching between fast and slow thinking. A leadership mindset is no longer a nice-to-have — it is a must-have.
  • Project experience is still hard currency that holds its value.
  • The value of specialized expertise is collapsing in the face of AI. Most MBA- or PhD-level knowledge is no longer a moat — it is a low step that most people can now climb over.
  • A lot of “iron rice bowl” skills — the kind that used to guarantee job security — are getting shattered by AI: building a polished PowerPoint deck, running Excel like a virtuoso. The new paradigm is HTML.
  • AI testing and evaluation will be one of the scarcest skills in the near future.

A Note to Enterprise Leaders and Investors

The first companies to wire tools like Figma, JIRA, cloud platforms, Outlook, and Teams directly into Claude Code will get something close to faster-than-light gains in enterprise efficiency — and that turns into a real competitive edge, fast.

  • Companies still stuck arguing over data security while keeping their employees’ hands tied risk getting lapped by competitors within a matter of months to a couple of years.
  • Internal AI training is probably the highest-ROI investment a company can make right now, and giving employees access to frontier AI tools is one of the fastest ways to boost short-term retention and loyalty. Nobody wants to work somewhere with a worse AI environment, even for more money or a bigger title, because nobody wants to sacrifice their future employability. Hiring FDEs externally right now comes at a steep premium — it is a cash-burning game that only the world’s top 100 companies can really afford. Most companies cannot compete on that basis, so finding the willing, capable people already inside the organization and investing in their training is the best short-term ROI available.
  • Under frameworks like MCP and A2A, the cost of cross-platform integration will keep falling. Going forward, user loyalty to products like SAP and Salesforce, and to the big three cloud platforms, will weaken.
  • Platforms like Claude Code, Codex, VS Code, and Cursor will gradually replace traditional work platforms like Windows and Office. Whoever ends up with the largest user base is a strong candidate to become the next tech giant — the next member of the “Magnificent Seven” (M7).
  • Right now, the share of employees who are genuinely fluent in AI is generally under 3%. The AI training market is enormous — and so is the market for training people to actually use the tools: selling shovels during a gold rush.
  • The concept of “human resources” is already behind the times. The new paradigm for Human Capital is Human + AI Agent Capital.

Reflecting on the FDE Experience

The most direct takeaway from this week was a new appreciation for how AI and humans relate to each other inside a real project — and for how human-to-human collaboration itself is changing.

The shared precondition underneath all of it: everyone on the team needs to know how to vibe code. Once that is in place, project velocity runs at least 3–5x faster than traditional development, and can peak north of 10x.

Is This the End? Not Even Close

AI still cannot fully operate Windows, macOS, Android, or the browser as an operating environment.

IoT integration still has a long way to go, too — smart cars, smart homes, smart communities, smart factories, smart robotics. There is an enormous amount of room left to build.

The shift has started. The operating model is not finished. Neither are we.
