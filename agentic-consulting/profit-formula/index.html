AI Is Rewriting Consulting's Profit Formula

For decades, consulting has been built on three kinds of scarcity: scarce knowledge, scarce talent, and scarce organizational experience. AI is rapidly eroding the first two — it can complete research, interview synthesis, preliminary analysis, deck drafting, and slide production in minutes. The real question is no longer "how do we use AI to work faster," but: when work that once took dozens of consultants several months can now be done by a small team with AI in a few weeks, what can a consulting firm still charge for — and why would a client still pay?

1. The Old Model Disrupted

What traditional consulting actually sells

The classic org structure is a talent pyramid: partners own client relationships, sales, and key judgment calls; project managers run delivery; a large base of junior consultants does research, interviews, analysis, modeling, and slide production.

Its economic model can be summarized as:

Revenue = Number of Consultants × Billable Hours × Rate
Profit = Revenue − Labor and Overhead Costs

The core operating metrics — utilization, bill rate, realization, partner leverage — all follow from this. Bigger projects, longer timelines, and more junior staff generally mean more revenue. Fixed fees, monthly retainers, and occasional value-share exist too, but fixed fees are usually still priced off estimated hours and staffing.

This model sells four things, in essence:

  1. Information and knowledge;
  2. A method for analyzing problems;
  3. Temporary, high-quality labor;
  4. The partner's credibility, judgment, and organizational endorsement.

The first three are the most exposed to AI.

The contradiction: the more efficient you get, the less you earn

The traditional formula is Revenue = People × Hours × Rate. Partner brand and methodology reputation set the rate; a large body of mid- and junior-level consultants does the research, analysis, modeling, coding, and slide-building; headcount sets sellable capacity. This made sense when expertise was scarce, information was expensive to gather, and complex delivery needed heavy coordination.

AI doesn't change the underlying demand for consulting — it changes the production function for a huge slice of the tasks inside it. Work that used to take days — draft code, architecture options, literature synthesis, first-pass analysis — can now take hours. It would be an overstatement to say marginal cost has gone to zero everywhere: inference, data governance, systems integration, validation, and accountability are still expensive. But the unit cost of replicable cognitive labor has dropped sharply — enough on its own to undercut the logic of billing by input.

This creates a triple squeeze: clients question headcount more easily; firms struggle to convert internal efficiency gains into revenue; generic frameworks and standard answers lose their scarcity value. If AI compresses a ten-day analysis into one day and the firm still bills by the day, higher efficiency means lower revenue; if it keeps billing for ten days, clients start to question the price. AI creates a fundamental contradiction: a firm's own efficiency gains can undermine its own billing basis. The businesses genuinely at risk aren't "all consulting" — they're pure-strategy decks, generic implementation, and staff augmentation.

Why AI is destabilizing the traditional model

The marginal cost of acquiring knowledge is collapsing. Industry research, competitor intelligence, regulatory filings, and best practices used to take consultants weeks to compile; clients can now do a first pass themselves with AI. The "I know something you don't" gap is harder and harder to charge a premium for.

Time invested and client value are decoupling. The contradiction above means the harder a firm works to compress delivery time, the more it risks compressing its own revenue.

The base of the talent pyramid is being compressed. Research, meeting notes, basic modeling, first drafts, and slide production are exactly the tasks that employ the most junior consultants and train them most intensively. Heavy automation here means fewer junior hires — and the loss of the traditional "learn while you deliver" pipeline that grows future partners.

Clients no longer just want an answer. AI can generate a large volume of plausible-sounding recommendations. What's genuinely scarce is judging which problem is worth solving, building organizational consensus, connecting to enterprise data and processes, driving implementation, and owning the outcome. Value is shifting from "producing answers" to "changing how the organization actually runs."

Clients expect to see value faster. The traditional sequence is research, then diagnose, then design, then implement. AI-native companies increasingly prefer to prototype quickly on real data, validate a use case in days or weeks, then decide whether to scale. Long upfront research phases and large-format reports are getting harder to price on their own.

None of this means consulting disappears. BCG's experiment with 758 consultants illustrates the boundary well: on generative-AI-suited product innovation tasks, participants using GPT-4 improved performance by roughly 40%; on complex business-judgment tasks that fell outside the model's capability boundary, performance actually dropped 23%. AI compresses the value of information processing while raising the value of problem framing, professional judgment, and ownership of outcomes.

2. The Future Consulting Model

Direction and shift

The future model isn't "the same project team plus an AI tool" — it requires five real shifts: from selling labor to selling verifiable outcomes; from one-time delivery to continuous operation; from a talent pyramid to a small senior team plus AI agents; from generic methodology to industry-specific workflow assets; from giving advice to sharing accountability for implementation.

From selling labor to selling verifiable outcomes

Clients stop buying ten consultants for twelve weeks and start buying a specific reduction in inventory, a shorter approval cycle, a lift in conversion rate, or a capability that genuinely goes live within a set timeframe. Pricing becomes composite: fixed fees for diagnosis and prototyping; subscription or usage-based fees for platforms, knowledge bases, and agents; managed-service fees for ongoing operation; outcome-based fees, or base-plus-upside, for anything cleanly attributable.

Outcome pricing doesn't fit every engagement. Strategic judgment calls and culture change are hard to attribute cleanly, so they need an agreed baseline, measurement window, list of confounding factors, and a cap on risk, set up front.

From one-time delivery to continuous operation

The future deliverable isn't just a report — it's a decision system, a knowledge agent, or a business process that keeps running. The firm doesn't just design it; it monitors performance, updates the knowledge base, manages model risk, and keeps optimizing. Revenue shifts from a one-time project fee to a blended structure of project fee + software fee + ongoing service fee.

From a talent pyramid to a small senior team plus AI agents

A typical team might be one business lead, one industry expert, one data/AI engineer, one change-management specialist, and a set of agents. Teams get smaller but need to understand business, data, technology, and organizational change simultaneously. Leverage no longer comes mainly from "how many junior consultants one partner manages," but from "how many data assets, workflows, and agents one senior team can reuse."

From generic methodology to industry workflow assets

Generic frameworks are easy for AI to replicate. What creates a real moat is process, data structure, regulatory requirements, evaluation criteria, and implementation experience embedded in a specific industry. Firms need to turn project experience into reusable assets — a procurement-negotiation agent, a bank compliance-investigation workflow, a drug-development knowledge system, a supply-chain exception-handling platform.

From giving advice to sharing accountability for implementation

The full loop for AI-native consulting should look like:

Identify the problem → Establish a baseline → Rapid prototype → Connect to real workflows → Run with humans and AI together → Measure results → Keep optimizing

The valuable deliverable isn't "advice on what the client should do" — it's the client already operating in the new way.

3. The New Business Model

The new profit formula

A more resilient formula for the future can be written as:

Revenue = IP × Customers × Recurring Usage × Outcome

This doesn't mean people stop mattering — it means people shift from measurable capacity to a high-leverage, scarce input. Real profit becomes: Profit = Revenue − AI/Platform Cost − Small Expert Team Cost. That produces a counterintuitive result: the most profitable firms may end up being the ones with the fewest people.

What the New Model Actually Sells

In one sentence: the new model sells certainty — not time, and not people.

Broken apart, it's really selling several layered things at once:

  • Verifiable outcomes — not "we did the analysis," but a measurable, attributable change: inventory down 30%, approval cycle cut from ten days to two.
  • Reusable assets — the industry agent, data model, or workflow itself becomes the product; clients buy something they can call on repeatedly, not a one-time recommendation.
  • Certainty of continuous operation — clients are buying the assurance that someone, or some system, keeps watching, maintaining, and owning the result over time, rather than a report that gets handed off and forgotten.
  • Transfer of risk and accountability — especially in compliance and governance settings, clients are really buying "someone else carries the blame if this goes wrong," which is an insurance-like premium.

Mapped onto the formula: Revenue = IP × Customers × Recurring Usage × Outcome. People haven't disappeared — they've shifted from measured capacity to a scarce, high-leverage input responsible for judgment, trust, and accountability, rather than for hours produced.

Five stages of business-model migration

Migration typically runs in five steps: consultants first use AI to boost project efficiency; they then codify repeated methods into IP; that IP gets packaged into a deployable platform or industry agent; the platform moves into continuous operation, generating subscription and managed-service revenue; finally, part of the fee gets tied to business outcomes. The five steps don't have to happen at once — but staying stuck at step one means the efficiency dividend is more likely to end up handed back to clients through price cuts.

The ideal business model for a solo consultant or small team

You don't need to start by building a large firm. One viable path:

You → Domain + Architecture → Reusable AI IP → serving multiple clients (A / B / C) simultaneously → consolidated into an AI Platform → moved into a Managed Service → collecting an Outcome Fee

A 5–10 person team can, in theory, serve a volume of work that once required dozens — even hundreds — of consultants. Revenue mix might look like: 20% advisory + 30% implementation + 30% recurring platform/managed service + 20% outcome/performance fee — a much healthier structure than relying on billable hours alone.

The five models below (the fifth split into three specific directions) illustrate how this path plays out in practice, each broken down by traditional model, future model, example, and pricing model.

① Outcome-Based / Value-Share Pricing

  • Traditional model: $300/hour × 10,000 hours = $3M. Revenue tracks hours worked, not the value the client actually captures.
  • Future model: Stop selling labor; tie fees to the client's KPI or ROI, with baseline, measurement window, and risk cap agreed up front, then take a share of the result.
  • Example: Cut claims-processing cost by 30% and take 15% of the savings. A client with $20M/year in claims-operations cost brought down to $5M via AI + automation pays the firm 20% × $5M = $1M/year — $5M over five years if the system keeps running, rather than a one-time $1M project fee. Straight-through-processing rates rising from 60% to 90% can also be billed on a tiered basis against the newly automated volume.
  • Pricing model: Performance fee, savings-share, gain-share. Since AI sharply shortens delivery time, net margin can be very high once the target result is hit.

② Consulting-as-a-Software / Vertical AI IP Packaging

  • Traditional model: A deck of dozens of slides and a one-time recommendation, with the methodology living in individual consultants' heads and slide libraries — hard to reuse.
  • Future model: Package accumulated methodology, industry data models, and governance rules into proprietary tools (agentic tools / internal copilots) — or an entire industry AI operating system.
  • Example: A Clinical Trial Intelligence Platform for life sciences (patient recruitment, site selection, protocol optimization, adverse-event intelligence, medical writing, regulatory intelligence, trial forecasting); an AI Credit Decision Platform for finance; an AI Claims Decision Platform for insurance; an AI Workforce Intelligence Platform for human capital; an AI Quality Intelligence Platform for manufacturing; an AI Asset Optimization Platform for energy.
  • Pricing model: Implementation fee + platform fee + usage fee + outcome fee. What ships isn't a report but an agent or pipeline actually running in the client's environment — marginal delivery cost approaches zero, yielding software-grade gross margins (80%+).

③ AI Managed Services

  • Traditional model: "We'll staff 50 engineers to run your SAP / cloud / infrastructure" — essentially day-rate or monthly-rate staff augmentation on-site.
  • Future model: "We'll give you an AI-operated finance / IT / supply chain / customer operations function." The client isn't buying 30 people — they're buying Digital Workforce as a Service: AI IT Operations, AI Finance Operations, AI Supply Chain Operations, AI Customer Service Operations, AI Cyber Operations, AI Data Operations.
  • Example: Deloitte's accounts-payable AI agent deployed for a consumer-goods company — 92% of invoices processed with no human touch, average processing time cut 50%–75%, while keeping human review, confidence scoring, and audit trails; Deloitte's Agentic AI offerings already explicitly include Agent Ops, Agent Managed Services, and Governance & Trust Monitoring.
  • Pricing model: $1M implementation + $200K/month managed service, instead of 100 consultants × $250/hour. Build once, get paid for years — and an AI agent's marginal cost runs far below a consultant's.

④ Risk-Bearing & Governance

  • Traditional model: Compliance review and security audits are typically an add-on to a project, still billed by the day, with fuzzy accountability.
  • Future model: Enterprises usually aren't short on technology — they're short on someone willing to own the risk of "we're afraid to actually use this." Provide "backstop" consulting covering AI compliance, security audits, hallucination governance, data privacy, and algorithmic accountability, taking on some of the governance risk directly.
  • Example: Governance & Trust Monitoring for a client's live AI agents — building monitoring and accountability before a compliance breach, hallucination, or data leak occurs.
  • Pricing model: A genuine insurance-like premium, priced against compliance-breach risk or the scale of potential business loss rather than hours worked.

⑤ Business-Focus Shift: From "Technical Implementation" to "High-Barrier Territory"

With AI able to handle most basic coding and generic architecture, high-margin technical consulting has to anchor in three areas AI can't solve in the short term:

Complex Systems Integration (Legacy)

  • Traditional model: Large teams billed by the day, manually working through decades-old COBOL/mainframe systems, or heavily coupled, undocumented systems.
  • Future model: Combine domain expertise with custom code-parsing agents to deliver low-risk, gradual modernization and data-governance work. AI can't reconstruct business logic it has no context for — which is exactly why consultants still matter here.
  • Example: Core-system modernization projects at large enterprises in finance, agribusiness, manufacturing, and biopharma.
  • Pricing model: Can still be tied to migration milestones or outcomes, but because it depends heavily on deep industry context and history, there's real room to negotiate price.

Physical AI / Edge Fusion (Physical AI)

  • Traditional model: Digital-only consulting largely stops at the software and data layer, leaving out physical-world uncertainty.
  • Future model: Composite architecture consulting spanning the digital and physical worlds — sim-to-real simulation, robotic control, vision-language-action (VLA) models, edge compute deployment, sensor fusion.
  • Example: Robotic-control projects tied to embodied AI, factory edge-compute deployment, and sensor-fusion programs.
  • Pricing model: A genuinely scarce capability with strong pricing power, usually billed as a composite architecture-consulting fee or long-term embedded engagement.

Organization & Culture Change (Change Mgmt)

  • Traditional model: Change management is often a supporting piece of a technical project, bundled into system delivery, and undervalued as a result.
  • Future model: The biggest obstacle to AI adoption is rarely the technology itself — it's misaligned org structure, unclear ownership, mismatched evaluation criteria, and broken processes. Future consulting needs to redesign workflows, redefine roles, and set an AI-adoption strategy — the kind of interpersonal and organizational design work AI genuinely can't replace.
  • Example: Designing an AI-adoption strategy, redefining roles and evaluation criteria, for a large enterprise organizational-change program.
  • Pricing model: A consulting fee tied to adoption rate and organizational-performance improvement, often paired with a long-term embedded service fee.

These three areas aren't new — what's changed is why they're a barrier. The reason used to be "enough people, enough experience, willingness to grind." Now it's context, cross-domain compound capability, and trust.

  • Complex Systems Integration: the barrier used to come from sheer headcount — a room full of consultants reading COBOL line by line. AI now handles a lot of basic code comprehension and generic architecture work, so the pure-headcount part is eroding. What's left is the combination of domain knowledge plus a custom code-parsing agent — knowing what context to feed the model and where it will misread the business logic. The barrier shifted from "willing to grind" to "knowing how to correctly give AI eyes."
  • Physical AI / Edge Fusion: this used to be classic engineering-consulting territory (robotics, embedded systems, automation), with the barrier coming from hardware-integration experience and control theory. LLMs and VLA models have now digitized the perceive-decide-act loop, adding an entirely new capability layer — sim-to-real, edge deployment — that used to belong separately to pure software firms or pure automation firms. Now you need both at once. The barrier shifted from "depth in one engineering discipline" to the scarcer intersection of digital AI capability and physical-systems capability.
  • Organization & Culture Change: change management is a classic consulting business, valuable for reducing resistance and redesigning process and incentives. What's different now is twofold — the content of change itself has shifted from "how do we optimize this process" to "how do we redistribute work, accountability, and roles alongside AI," a genuinely new topic; and the pace has shifted too, since AI capability can make a role's rationale obsolete within months, forcing organizations to adapt far faster than before. That raises the value, and urgency, of exactly the interpersonal and organizational-design skill AI can't replace.

In short: the names of these three areas haven't changed, but the part of them that AI can standardize has already been eaten. What's left — the genuinely valuable part — has gotten narrower and far more dependent on context and cross-domain capability. That's exactly why they're now called out separately as the high-barrier territory AI can't solve in the short term.

4. Leaders Are Already Moving

Outcome pricing is moving from idea to contract. BCG's CEO has said roughly three-quarters of its largest AI engagements now use variable-fee arrangements. A separate BCG survey found that GenAI clients on outcome-based models reported cost savings at a higher rate than clients on fixed pricing (84% vs. 67%). This doesn't prove outcome-based pricing always works, but it shows the market is genuinely willing to test tying vendor return to client value.

Assets and continuous service are becoming growth engines. Accenture reported FY2025 generative and agentic AI revenue of $2.7B and related bookings of $5.9B, with $800M invested in R&D assets, platforms, and industry/functional solutions. Deloitte now explicitly offers Agent Ops, Agent Managed Services, and Governance & Trust Monitoring. Together, these point toward a "consulting + software + managed operation" combination rather than simply adding more consultants.

A 2026 McKinsey survey found that 40% of large enterprises are already using AI agents at scale, and 32% said they've decided to build in-house software they might otherwise have bought, specifically because of coding agents. Meanwhile, recent Stanford research suggests AI is pushing consulting from time-based pricing toward hybrid, subscription, outcome-based, and asset-based models.

Several important pieces of the puzzle have already emerged:

  • Accenture — proving AI-consulting demand has reached scale. Generative and agentic AI revenue hit $2.7B (3x year over year), with bookings of $5.9B (nearly double). This proves demand, but doesn't prove traditional consulting's delivery and pricing model has fully changed.
  • McKinsey Lilli — turning individual experience into organizational infrastructure. McKinsey connected its internal knowledge base to Lilli; roughly 72% of staff actively use it, with time savings up to 30% on search and synthesis work, and over 500,000 queries handled monthly. More importantly, McKinsey is starting to bring similar architecture to clients — consulting assets are moving from document libraries to callable, continuously updated intelligent systems.
  • Palantir's AIP Bootcamp — deliver a result first, then sell long-term capability. Palantir isn't a traditional consulting firm, but it may be closer to the future shape of one. It puts software, engineers, an industry problem, and client data into a short bootcamp so clients can experience a working solution on their own use case within days, then expands into a long-term platform relationship — a new way to acquire clients by delivering a real result first.
  • Deloitte's Zora AI — moving from advice to continuous operation. Deloitte deployed an AI agent for accounts-payable processing at a consumer-goods company: 92% of invoices processed with no human touch, average processing time cut 50%–75%, while retaining human review, confidence scoring, and audit trails — a typical combination of "industry knowledge + software asset + human oversight + continuous operation."
  • Intercom's Fin — a nearby example of outcome-based pricing. Intercom isn't a consulting firm, but Fin doesn't charge by seat or by conversation — it charges for customer issues successfully resolved. That model generated tens of millions of dollars in revenue in under a year and now resolves over a million customer-service conversations a week — proof that when a result can be defined and verified, outcome-based pricing isn't just workable, it genuinely aligns vendor and client incentives.

Together, these cases suggest success isn't "employees started using AI" — it's a change to at least one of: delivery speed, deliverable format, how the client operates, revenue structure, or how risk is shared.

Google Cloud and Accenture have already started embedding forward-deployed AI engineers directly inside client organizations — effectively linking "consulting → implementation → AI workforce" into one continuous chain.

6. How Partners Should Transform

Partners have to be the ones actively cannibalizing the old model. You can't ask a team to be ten times more efficient while still measuring success by headcount and billable hours. Seven things matter most:

  1. Go narrow and deep, not broad and shallow — focus on a handful of high-value, repeatable, measurable industry workflows rather than selling "AI strategy" broadly. Depth is what turns experience into a real asset.
  2. Build a small, cross-functional standing team — one business lead + one industry expert + one AI engineer + one change specialist + a set of agents, replacing a large base of junior consultants adding headcount.
  3. Turn project knowledge into a firm-owned, governable asset — after every engagement, ask whether what was learned can become a template, an eval set, or an agent. Knowledge left scattered across laptops and decks is largely wasted.
  4. Build composite contract structures — fixed fees for diagnosis/prototyping, platform/subscription fees for ongoing use, managed-service fees for operation, and outcome-share for anything attributable. A single pricing mechanism is no longer enough.
  5. Build measurement and governance capability — charging for outcomes requires being able to define a baseline, a measurement window, and rule out confounding factors. That's a new capability in its own right, not just a new contract clause.
  6. Establish rigorous model evaluation, human review, data security, and clear accountability boundaries — the more capable the AI, the less blurry professional accountability can be.
  7. Rework partner evaluation — move it from contract value and utilization toward client outcomes, recurring revenue, asset-reuse rate, and client adoption.

A partner's most important skill going forward isn't selling projects — it's choosing which problems are worth productizing, designing a new economics of shared upside, and getting clients to genuinely co-own the change.

7. How Employees Should Transform

Employees shouldn't aim to "out-research and out-slide the AI" — that's a fight that's hard to win. The more valuable direction is becoming a professional who can direct, verify, and operationalize AI. Five capabilities matter most:

  • Problem framing: turning a fuzzy management problem into testable hypotheses and business metrics.
  • Professional judgment: spotting a model's limits, evidence gaps, and plausible-sounding errors — knowing where AI can be trusted and where it will quietly get things wrong.
  • AI orchestration: making models, data, tools, and human review work together as a stable workflow.
  • Business execution: genuinely understanding client processes, organizational politics, and resistance to change.
  • Asset-building instinct: treating every piece of analysis as a candidate for becoming a template, a data structure, an eval set, or an agent — not a one-off deliverable.

The growth path for junior consultants has to change too. It used to be: do a lot of foundational work first, then gradually get closer to the client and to decisions. Going forward it should be: get onto the ground with the client sooner, understand their processes sooner, and use AI to own end-to-end tasks earlier. The standard for evaluating employees should move from "how much time did they put in" to "how complex a problem did they solve independently, and what reusable asset did they leave behind."

In short: firms need to shift from selling labor to managing assets, and individuals need to shift from producing answers to designing and supervising a system that keeps producing answers.

Closing: Consulting Won't Disappear — But "Selling Time" Will Keep Losing Value

AI hasn't eliminated the client's need for outside help. If anything, the faster technology changes, the more enterprises need trustworthy judgment, cross-functional coordination, and implementation capability. But clients are increasingly unwilling to pay a premium for moving information around, basic analysis, or a large project team as such.

The genuinely competitive consulting firm of the future won't be the one with the most consultants — it will be the one that can turn a small senior team, the best industry assets, and the most reliable AI systems into a verifiable business result, fastest.

Consulting used to sell "the time of smart people." In the future it will sell "results for which people, data, and agents jointly take responsibility."

So what's genuinely worth building isn't a smarter consulting team — it's a consulting firm that keeps looking less and less like a consulting firm — moving from consulting, to AI engineering, to an AI platform, to a managed service, and finally to outcome sharing.

Appendix A: The Most Dangerous Business Going Forward

The most dangerous thing in the AI era isn't "traditional consulting getting replaced by AI" — it's "a firm's delivery efficiency going up while its pricing logic is still selling days." Specifically, these categories are the most exposed:

  • Strategy-only: "helping the CEO define an AI strategy." AI will compress this business dramatically.
  • Generic implementation: "deploying Salesforce / ServiceNow / Azure / AWS for you." Coding agents plus vendor professional services will eat a large share of this value — a recent McKinsey study found roughly a third of enterprises have already chosen to build software in-house that they might otherwise have bought, specifically because of agentic coding capability.
  • Staff augmentation: "here are 20 data scientists" is the category with the least upside, because AI sharply raises individual productivity — clients naturally start asking why they need 20 people at all.
  • PPT consulting: likely the category hit hardest by AI, because research → synthesis → slides → recommendation is exactly the kind of work LLMs are best at.

Technical consulting that stays genuinely high-margin will gradually move along People → IP → Platform → Outcome → Recurring Revenue — from selling expert time to selling replicable technical assets plus ongoing business results.

Appendix B: From $10M–100M Projects to an AI-Native MVP Team

These seven project types share a common structure: in a traditional team, over 70% of headcount is doing "volume work" — reading code, cleaning data, writing tests, translating training material — while less than 30% is genuinely scarce judgment: architecture decisions, regulatory interpretation, handling change resistance, client trust. AI compresses exactly that 70%, which is why the MVP team composition is almost a fixed pattern: one chief architect, one or two domain experts, two AI/platform engineers, one change/operations lead, and one client-relationship/risk lead. The real ceiling on team size comes from pure bandwidth constraints — how many regions or business units need to run in parallel at once — and when that's the actual bottleneck, a small team should say so honestly rather than force-fit the model.

Why a Small Team Is More Profitable

What a large team used to do splits into three categories of work:

  1. Replicable cognitive labor — reading code, mapping undocumented systems, writing tests, reconciling data. This scales with headcount, because each person can only process so much information.
  2. Scarce judgment — architecture decisions, risk tradeoffs, building client trust. This was always done by a handful of people; adding headcount never helped here.
  3. Coordination overhead — a large team needs a large amount of labor just to manage itself: project managers, alignment meetings, handoff documentation. This is a byproduct of team size, not something the client actually wanted.

AI compresses category 1. Category 2 doesn't change. Category 3 simply disappears with a small team — that's a net gain, not a tradeoff. A small team can substitute for a large one exactly when category 1 dominates a project's real difficulty. If a project's core difficulty is pure parallel bandwidth — say, 20 workflows running simultaneously across 8 regions — a small team genuinely can't do it, and should say so rather than force the fit.

Because category-1 work no longer scales cost with headcount while the value delivered to the client stays the same, the margin structure improves on three fronts at once: fewer people to pay; the same or a higher price justified by certainty rather than hours; and reusable tooling and playbooks that amortize across clients, continuing to compress marginal delivery cost.

How to Get Mid-to-Large Enterprises to Buy In

On the selling side, five moves matter most:

  1. De-risk with a low-stakes pilot — sell a 2–4 week diagnostic/prototype (Palantir Bootcamp-style) so the client sees a working result on their own real data before committing to a large contract. Buying "verified certainty" is a far easier decision than buying a promise.
  2. The partner's personal credibility is the core sales asset — without a brand to lean on, a small team sells on "what has this person actually done." Treat conference talks, case studies, and a visible track record as brand equity worth actively building.
  3. Bypass traditional IT procurement, go straight to the C-suite — systems transformation is a strategic-level problem; go directly to the CIO/CTO/COO rather than through a standard vendor RFP process, which is structurally biased against small teams.
  4. Ally with large SIs and the Big Four instead of competing with them — position as "the hardest 20% of technical work the big firms can't move fast on," subcontracting to borrow their procurement channel and compliance backing while keeping the deepest technical slice for yourself.
  5. Risk-sharing contract terms lower the client's decision cost — tie part of the fee to milestones or outcomes, include failure-refund clauses, carry professional liability insurance — shifting some of the decision risk from the client onto yourself in exchange for an easier signature.

On the buying side — what a client should actually demand before believing it:

As a buyer, you shouldn't trust any claim of "we can do this." You should require it to be demonstrated under low-risk, reversible conditions before betting a large scope on it:

  1. Insist on a low-risk pilot with a clean exit, not a big contract up front — a 2–4 week diagnostic/prototype phase that runs on your real systems and real data, producing something that actually runs (a migrated module, a dependency graph, a successful strangler-fig pilot), not a slide deck. You retain the full right to walk away afterward with no further obligation.
  2. Ask to see actual work artifacts from past projects, not case-study narratives — "show me the code-parsing report AI generated on your last similar project," "show me how much of your migration scripts were AI-generated versus human-written." If they can only tell stories and can't produce concrete artifacts, the methodology probably isn't real — it's improvisation.
  3. Call a past client's technical lead directly, not a logo wall — don't ask "were you satisfied," ask "if the cutover had gone wrong, who was on the front line, and how fast was the response?" or "which part of this made you most nervous?" Anyone can put a logo on a slide; specific operational recall is much harder to fabricate.
  4. Require the AI decision chain to be auditable — ask explicitly which steps are AI-decided and which require mandatory human review, and what the accountability chain is if an AI-generated migration plan causes a problem. A responsible team can draw this chain clearly rather than gesture vaguely at "AI-assisted."
  5. Pay in milestone-gated tranches, not one large upfront contract — define verifiable acceptance criteria at each stage; if a stage fails acceptance, the project and the payments both stop. Your downside is always capped at "the next milestone," not the whole project's risk at once.

Warning signs worth watching for: a team that only talks about "how efficient we are" and never states what part of the project it genuinely can't do; a team that resists a low-risk pilot and insists on a full contract upfront; case studies that cite only outcomes ("we improved X by 30%") with no technical specifics or lessons learned — often a repackaged narrative rather than real experience.

The one-line answer: don't trust the claim itself — require it to become something visible, under small-scope, low-risk, reversible conditions, before handing over a large-scope bet. Any team that resists this verification process and asks for trust before evidence is itself a red flag.

Methodology for Complex Systems Integration & Transformation

  • AI-assisted systems archaeology — use code-parsing agents to quickly map business-logic boundaries, hidden dependencies, and data lineage in legacy systems, compressing months of manual mapping into weeks.
  • Strangler-fig migration — replace in stages rather than attempting a big-bang rewrite; each stage is independently reversible, cutting risk into small pieces.
  • Run technical migration and change management in parallel, not sequentially — organizational acceptance, not technical completion, is usually the real reason migrations fail.
  • Stay embedded post-cutover rather than "deliver and leave" — most migration risk materializes in the six months after cutover; the managed-operations phase is part of the delivery methodology itself, not an add-on service.

AI 正在重写咨询业的利润公式

过去几十年，咨询业建立在三种稀缺性之上：知识稀缺、人才稀缺和组织经验稀缺。AI 正在迅速削弱前两种稀缺性——它可以在几分钟内完成资料检索、访谈整理、数据初步分析、方案生成和演示文稿制作。于是，咨询业面临的根本问题不再是"如何用AI提高效率"，而是：当原来需要几十名顾问、持续数月才能完成的工作，现在可以由一个小团队借助AI在数周内完成，咨询公司还能按什么收费？客户又为什么愿意付费？

一、传统咨询行业的商业模式被AI颠覆

1. 传统咨询卖的究竟是什么

传统咨询的典型组织形式是人才金字塔：合伙人负责客户关系、销售和关键判断，项目经理负责组织交付，大量年轻顾问承担研究、访谈、分析、建模和材料制作。

它的经济模型可以概括为：

收入 = 顾问人数 × 可计费时间 × 单位费率
利润 = 收入 − 人力成本及管理成本

因此，传统咨询公司的核心经营指标通常包括人员利用率、项目费率、收费实现率和合伙人杠杆率。项目越大、周期越长、投入的初级顾问越多，收入通常越高。除了按人天收费，行业也采用固定项目费、月度顾问费和少量价值分成，但固定项目费背后仍经常以预计工时和人员配置为定价基础。

这一模式本质上出售四种东西：

  1. 信息和知识；
  2. 分析问题的方法；
  3. 临时增加的高质量人力；
  4. 合伙人的信誉、判断与组织背书。

其中，前三项最容易受到AI冲击。

2. 传统模式的矛盾：效率越高，收入反而越低

传统咨询的基本公式是 Revenue = People × Hours × Rate。合伙人品牌、方法论声誉和客户关系决定费率，大量中初级顾问完成研究、分析、建模、编码和材料制作，人力规模决定可销售产能。这个模式曾经合理，因为专业知识稀缺、信息获取昂贵、复杂交付需要大量协调。

AI 改变的不是咨询需求本身，而是其中一大批任务的生产函数。过去需要数天完成的代码草稿、架构选项、资料综述或分析初稿，现在可能在数小时内完成。这里不宜夸张为"全部边际成本归零"：模型推理、数据治理、系统集成、验证与责任承担仍然昂贵。但可复制认知劳动的单位成本显著下降，已经足以动摇按投入计价的合理性。

这造成三重压力：客户更容易质疑人天规模；咨询公司难以把内部效率红利转化为收入；通用框架和标准答案的稀缺性下降。如果AI把十天的分析压缩为一天，而咨询公司仍按人天收费，效率越高，收入反而越少；如果继续按原来的十天收费，客户又会质疑价格。AI由此制造了一个根本矛盾：咨询公司的效率提升，可能破坏自身的计费基础。真正危险的业务因此不是所有咨询，而是纯策略 PPT、通用实施与人员增补。

3. AI为什么会动摇传统咨询模式

知识获取的边际成本迅速下降。 行业资料、竞争对手信息、政策文件和最佳实践，过去需要顾问花费数周搜集整理，现在客户自己也可以借助AI完成初步研究。"我知道而你不知道"的知识差，越来越难支撑高额溢价。

时间投入与客户价值开始脱钩。 效率提升与计费基础之间的矛盾（见上）意味着，越努力压缩交付时间，越可能压缩自己的收入。

人才金字塔的底层工作被压缩。 资料研究、会议纪要、基础建模、报告初稿和幻灯片制作，恰恰是年轻顾问数量最多、训练最集中的工作。如果这些任务被大幅自动化，咨询公司不仅需要更少的初级员工，也会失去传统的"边做项目、边培养下一代合伙人"的人才机制。

客户需要的不再只是一份答案。 AI可以生成大量看起来合理的建议。真正稀缺的逐渐变成：判断哪个问题值得解决、获得组织共识、接入企业数据和流程、推动方案落地，并对最终结果负责。咨询价值因此从"生产答案"转向"改变组织的实际运行结果"。

客户开始期待更快看到价值。 传统模式通常先调研、再诊断、再设计、最后实施。AI原生企业更倾向于先用真实数据快速做出原型，在数天或数周内验证一个场景，再决定是否扩大投入。冗长的前期研究和大部头报告，将越来越难被单独定价。

这并不意味着咨询会消失。BCG对758名顾问的实验恰好说明了其中的边界：在适合生成式AI的产品创新任务中，使用GPT-4的参与者表现提高约40%；但在超出模型能力边界的复杂商业判断任务中，表现反而下降23%。AI会压缩信息加工的价值，却会提高问题定义、专业判断和结果责任的价值。

二、未来的咨询模式

1. 方向和趋势转变

新的咨询模式不会只是"传统项目组加一个AI工具"，而应当发生五个转变：从出售人力转向出售可验证的结果；从一次性交付转向持续运营；从人才金字塔转向"小型高级团队＋AI智能体"；从通用方法论转向行业工作流资产；从提出建议转向共同承担实施责任。

2. 从出售人力转向出售可验证的结果

客户购买的不再是十名顾问工作十二周，而是库存降低多少、审批周期缩短多少、销售转化率提升多少，或者某项能力在多长时间内真正上线。新的收费方式可能是组合式的：诊断和原型阶段采用固定费用；平台、知识库和智能体按订阅量或使用量收费；持续运营采用托管服务费；可清晰归因的场景采用按结果收费，或基础费用加收益分成。

结果收费并非适合所有项目。战略判断、文化变革等工作的成果很难准确归因，因此需要事先约定基线、衡量周期、外部影响因素和风险上限。

3. 从一次性交付转向持续运营

未来的咨询成果不应只是一份报告，而可能是一套持续运行的决策系统、知识智能体或业务流程。咨询公司不仅帮助客户设计它，还要持续监测效果、更新知识、管理模型风险和优化流程。收入也会从一次性项目费，转向"项目费＋软件费＋持续服务费"的复合结构。

4. 从人才金字塔转向"小型高级团队＋AI智能体"

典型团队可能由一名业务负责人、一名行业专家、一名数据或AI工程师、一名变革管理专家和一组智能体组成。团队规模变小，但需要同时理解业务、数据、技术和组织变革。杠杆不再主要来自"一个合伙人管理多少名年轻顾问"，而来自"一个高级团队能够复用多少数据资产、工作流和智能体"。

5. 从通用方法论转向行业工作流资产

通用框架很容易被AI复制。真正形成壁垒的，是嵌入具体行业的流程、数据结构、监管要求、评价标准和实施经验。咨询公司需要把项目经验沉淀为可重复使用的资产，例如采购谈判智能体、银行合规调查流程、药物研发知识系统或供应链异常处理平台。

6. 从提出建议转向共同承担实施责任

AI原生咨询的完整闭环应当是：

识别问题 → 建立基线 → 快速原型 → 接入真实流程 → 人机协作运行 → 衡量结果 → 持续优化

真正有价值的交付物，不是"建议客户应该做什么"，而是客户已经开始以新的方式运行。

三、新的商业模式

新利润公式

未来更有韧性的公式可以写成：

Revenue = IP × Customers × Recurring Usage × Outcome

这不是说人不重要，而是人的作用从可计量产能变成高杠杆稀缺输入。真正的利润则是：Profit = Revenue − AI/Platform Cost − Small Expert Team Cost。于是出现一个反直觉的现象——最赚钱的咨询公司，可能恰恰是"人最少"的公司。

新的模式出售的是什么

如果只用一句话概括：新模式卖的是"确定性"，而不是"时间"或"人"。

具体拆开来看，新模式出售的是几层叠加的东西：

  • 可验证的结果——不是"我们分析过了"，而是"库存降了30%""审批周期从10天缩到2天"这种可衡量、可归因的变化。
  • 可复用的资产——行业Agent、数据模型、工作流本身变成了产品，客户买的是能反复调用的东西，不是一次性的建议。
  • 持续运营的确定性——买的是"这件事有人/有系统在长期看着、维护着、对结果负责"，而不是一份报告后就散场。
  • 风险与责任的转移——尤其在合规、治理场景里，客户买的其实是"出问题有人担着"，这是保险属性的溢价。

对应到公式上：Revenue = IP × Customers × Recurring Usage × Outcome。人没有消失，但人从"被计量的产能"变成了"稀缺的高杠杆输入"——负责判断、信任建立、和承担责任，而不是负责产出工时。

商业模式迁移的五个阶段

商业模式迁移通常分为五步：顾问先用 AI 提升项目效率；再把重复方法沉淀为 IP；随后将 IP 封装为可部署平台或行业 Agent；平台进入持续运营，形成订阅和托管收入；最后把部分费用与业务结果绑定。五步不必一次完成，但如果始终停留在第一步，效率红利最终更可能被客户通过降价拿走。

真正的超级利润，来自"咨询 + 软件"的组合。传统 SaaS 是软件产品由客户自己实施，传统咨询是靠人帮客户实施；未来的赢家则是 IP / Software + AI Agents + Consultants 三者的组合——Consultants 不再是"生产资料"，而是变成销售、架构、domain expertise 与 transformation leadership 的承担者，大量执行工作则由 AI agents、软件与可复用 IP 完成。业务流程接入 AI Platform / IP 后，Agents 与 Data 共同驱动 KPI / Outcome，最终转化为 Recurring Revenue。

下面五种模式正是按照这座金字塔从上到下的顺序排列——最先出场的，就是最靠近顶端的模式。

未来最理想的个人/小团队商业模式

甚至不需要一开始就建立大型咨询公司。一个可行的路径是：

你 → Domain + Architecture → Reusable AI IP → 同时服务多个客户（Client A / B / C）→ 沉淀为 AI Platform → 进入 Managed Service → 收取 Outcome Fee

一个 5–10 人的团队，理论上可以服务过去需要几十、甚至上百名顾问才能覆盖的业务量。收入结构可能变成：20% advisory + 30% implementation + 30% recurring platform/managed service + 20% outcome/performance fee——这比单纯依赖 billable hours 健康得多。

下面五种模式（其中第五种下分三个具体方向）分别说明了这一路径在不同场景下的落地方式，每种都从传统模式、未来的模式、例子、收费模式四个角度展开。

① 结果导向/价值分成计费（Outcome-Based / Value-Share Pricing）

  • 传统模式：$300/hour × 10,000 hours = $3M，收入与投入工时挂钩，与客户实际获得的价值无关。
  • 未来的模式：不再卖人力，而是绑定客户的 KPI 或 ROI，事先约定基线、衡量周期和风险上限，再按结果提成。
  • 例子：把 claims processing cost 降低 30%，按节省金额的 15% 分成；客户原本每年 $20M 的 claims operations cost，通过 AI + automation 降到 $5M，咨询公司抽成 20% × $5M = $1M/年，若系统持续运行五年即为 $5M，而不是一次性 $1M 的项目费。也可以按 STP（一次通过率）从 60% 提升到 90% 后新增的自动处理量阶梯收费。
  • 收费模式：绩效费、节省金额分成、收益共享；由于 AI 极大缩短了交付周期，一旦达到既定结果，净利润率可以非常高。

② "咨询即软件"与资产化（Consulting-as-a-Software / Vertical AI IP Packaging）

  • 传统模式：交付几十页 PPT 和一次性建议，方法论留在顾问的个人经验和幻灯片里，难以复用。
  • 未来的模式：把过去沉淀的咨询方法论、行业数据模型和治理规范，封装成内部专有工具（Agentic Tools / Internal Copilots），甚至直接封装为一整套行业 AI Operating System。
  • 例子：Life Sciences 的 Clinical Trial Intelligence Platform（患者招募、选点、方案优化、不良事件情报、医学写作、监管情报、试验预测）；金融的 AI Credit Decision Platform；保险的 AI Claims Decision Platform；Human Capital 的 AI Workforce Intelligence Platform；制造的 AI Quality Intelligence Platform；能源的 AI Asset Optimization Platform。
  • 收费模式：Implementation fee + Platform fee + Usage fee + Outcome fee。交付给客户的不再是报告，而是直接在客户环境中运行的行业 Agent 或流水线，边际交付成本接近于零，从而实现软件级别的毛利率（80%+）。

③ AI Managed Services

  • 传统模式："我们派 50 个工程师帮你运营 SAP / Cloud / Infrastructure"，本质是人天/人月计费的驻场外包。
  • 未来的模式："我们给你一个 AI-operated finance / IT / supply chain / customer operations"——客户买的不是 30 个人，而是一整套 Digital Workforce as a Service，涵盖 AI IT Operations、AI Finance Operations、AI Supply Chain Operations、AI Customer Service Operations、AI Cyber Operations、AI Data Operations。
  • 例子：Deloitte 为一家消费品公司部署的应付账款处理 AI 智能体，92% 的发票实现无人工接触处理，单张发票处理时间平均减少 50%–75%，同时保留人工审核、置信度和审计追踪；Deloitte 的 Agentic AI 服务中也已明确包括 Agent Ops、Agent Managed Services、Governance & Trust Monitoring。
  • 收费模式：$1M implementation + $200K/month managed service，而不是 100 consultants × $250/hour。一次开发、多年收费，且 AI agent 的边际成本远低于顾问。

④ 结果兜底与风险共担（Risk-Bearing & Governance）

  • 传统模式：合规审查、安全审计通常是项目的附属环节，仍按人天收费，责任边界模糊。
  • 未来的模式：企业并不缺技术，缺的是"不敢用、怕出事"时的责任承担者。提供涵盖 AI 合规、安全审计、幻觉治理、数据隐私与算法责任的"兜底式"咨询，承担部分风险治理责任。
  • 例子：为企业上线的 AI agent 提供 Governance & Trust Monitoring，在合规违约、模型幻觉或数据泄露风险发生前建立监控与追责机制。
  • 收费模式：属于典型的保险属性溢价（Insurance-like Premium），定价脱离劳动力时长，直接挂钩合规违约风险或商业损失规模。

② vs ③ 的区别是什么

这两个很容易混，但核心区别是：②卖的是产品本身，③卖的是持续运营这件事。

②咨询即软件/资产化③AI Managed Services
卖什么行业Agent/平台/IP本身——一个可以被多个客户使用的软件资产一项"持续帮你把某个业务功能运营好"的服务
可复制性高——同一套Clinical Trial Intelligence Platform可以卖给多家药企，边际成本趋近于零相对低——每个客户的运营通常还是相对独立、定制化部署，虽然底层可能用同一套Agent技术
客户拿到什么一个自己（或半自主）能用的软件/系统，本质是"给你一把好用的工具"不需要自己动手，"你不用管，我们的AI+人来帮你管这件事"
商业本质更像做软件公司——implementation fee + platform fee + usage fee + outcome fee，追求软件级毛利率（80%+）更像做运营外包的AI升级版——一次implementation费+按月managed service费，追求经常性收入和客户黏性
护城河来源产品本身的行业深度和跨客户网络效应运营质量、SLA可靠性、以及"你不敢自己接手"的粘性

打个比方：②是"卖你一台洗衣机"，③是"我每周上门帮你洗衣服"。有意思的是这两者经常结合——Deloitte的Zora AI应付账款案例，本质上是先做了一个②式的Agent平台，再以③的方式持续运营交付，这也是文章里"迁移路径"最终会走到的地方：Platform → Managed Service 是承接关系，不是互斥关系。

⑤ 业务聚焦转移：从"技术实施"向"高壁垒场景"迁移

在 AI 能够处理大多数基础编码与通用架构的背景下，高利润的技术咨询必须锚定在 AI 短期内无法解决的三个方向：

复杂系统集成（Legacy）

  • 传统模式：大量顾问按人天投入，逐行梳理运行数十年的 COBOL/Mainframe 或严重耦合、无文档的系统。
  • 未来的模式：结合领域专家知识（Domain Knowledge）与定制化代码解析 Agent，提供低风险、平滑演进的现代化架构迁移与数据治理服务；AI 难以直接凭空重构没有上下文的业务逻辑，这恰恰保留了顾问的价值。
  • 例子：金融、农业、制造、生物医药等行业中大型企业的核心系统现代化迁移项目。
  • 收费模式：仍可与迁移里程碑或结果挂钩，但因高度依赖行业 context 与历史知识，议价空间大。

物理AI/边缘融合（Physical AI）

  • 传统模式：数字化咨询的能力边界基本止步于软件与数据层面，不覆盖物理世界的不确定性。
  • 未来的模式：跨越数字与物理世界的复合型架构咨询，涵盖 Sim-to-Real 仿真、机器人控制、视觉-语言-动作（VLA）模型、边缘算力部署、传感器融合。
  • 例子：结合具身智能的机器人控制项目、工厂边缘算力部署与传感器融合方案。
  • 收费模式：属于高度稀缺资源，具备极强的溢价能力，通常以复合型架构咨询费或长期驻场合作形式计费。

组织与文化变革（Change Mgmt）

  • 传统模式：变更管理常作为技术项目的辅助环节，附属于系统交付，价值容易被低估。
  • 未来的模式：阻碍 AI 落地最大的往往不是技术本身，而是组织架构、权责分配、考核标准与业务流程的错位；未来的咨询需要帮客户重构工作流（Workflow Redesign）、重新定义岗位职责、制定技术采用战略——这种人际博弈与组织设计是 AI 完全无法替代的。
  • 例子：为大型企业设计 AI Adoption 战略、重新划分岗位职责与考核标准的组织变革项目。
  • 收费模式：与采纳率、组织效能改善挂钩的咨询费，常搭配长期陪伴式的服务费。

高壁垒场景以前就存在，现在和以前不一样的地方在于："为什么有壁垒"的原因变了——从"人多、经验多、肯啃苦活"，变成了"context、跨领域复合能力、和信任"。

  • 复杂系统集成（Legacy）：以前壁垒主要来自人力规模——一堆顾问一行行读COBOL代码、手工梳理文档缺失的系统。现在AI已经能处理大量基础的代码理解和通用架构工作，纯粹靠人力堆砌的部分正在被侵蚀。真正剩下的壁垒变成了"领域知识+定制化代码解析Agent"的组合能力——知道该给AI喂什么context、知道哪里的业务逻辑AI会理解错。壁垒从"肯干"变成了"知道怎么正确地给AI装上眼睛"。
  • 物理AI/边缘融合（Physical AI）：以前这一直是工程咨询的领域（机器人、嵌入式系统、自动化），壁垒来自硬件集成经验和控制理论。现在LLM/VLA（视觉-语言-动作模型）把"感知-决策-控制"这条链路数字化了，新增了一整层原来不存在的能力需求——sim-to-real仿真、边缘算力部署这些原来纯软件公司或纯自动化公司各管一段的东西，现在需要同时懂两边。壁垒从"单一工程学科深度"变成了"数字AI能力+物理系统能力"的交叉稀缺性。
  • 组织与文化变革（Change Mgmt）：以前变革管理是经典咨询业务，价值来自减少变革阻力、重新设计流程和考核。现在不一样的地方在于两点——一是变革的内容本身变了，不再只是"怎么优化流程"，而是"怎么和AI一起重新分配工作、责任、和岗位"，这是全新的议题；二是变革的速度要求变了，AI能力可能在几个月内让一个岗位的存在意义发生根本变化，组织必须以比以前快得多的节奏去调整，这对"人际博弈+组织设计"这类AI无法替代的能力，反而提出了更高、更紧迫的要求。

一句话总结：这三个领域的名字没变，但里面能被AI标准化掉的那部分已经被吃掉了，剩下的、真正值钱的部分变得更窄、更依赖context和跨领域能力——这也是为什么它们现在被单独挑出来，作为"AI短期内解决不了"的高壁垒方向。

四、头部咨询公司在行动

结果定价正在从理念进入合同。 BCG CEO 表示，其最大型 AI 项目中约四分之三采用可变费用安排。另一项 BCG 调查发现，采用结果导向商业模式的 GenAI 客户报告成本节约的比例高于固定价格客户（84% 对 67%）。这不能证明结果收费必然成功，却说明市场愿意尝试把供应商回报与客户价值绑定。

资产和持续服务正在成为增长引擎。 Accenture 披露 FY2025 生成式及 Agentic AI 收入为 27 亿美元、相关订单为 59 亿美元，并投入 8 亿美元研发资产、平台及行业和职能解决方案。Deloitte 则已明确提供 Agent Ops、Agent Managed Services 和 Governance & Trust Monitoring。这些动作共同指向"咨询 + 软件 + 托管运营"的组合，而非单纯增加顾问人数。

2026 年 McKinsey 的调研显示，40% 的大型企业已经在规模化使用 AI agents，而且 32% 的企业表示，已经因为 coding agents 而决定内部自己构建原本可能购买的软件功能。与此同时，Stanford 近期的研究指出，AI 会推动咨询从 time-based pricing 向 hybrid、subscription、outcome-based 和 asset-based 模式迁移。

市场上已经出现了几块重要拼图：

  • Accenture：证明AI咨询需求已经形成规模。 生成式和智能体AI相关收入达27亿美元（同比三倍），相关订单达59亿美元（接近翻倍）。这证明了市场需求，但不等于传统咨询的交付和收费模式已经全部改变。
  • McKinsey Lilli：把个人经验变成组织基础设施。 McKinsey将内部知识接入Lilli，平台推广后约72%的员工活跃使用，在信息搜索和综合工作上最多节省30%的时间，每月处理超过50万次提问。更重要的是，McKinsey开始把类似架构带给客户——咨询资产正在从文档库转变为可调用、可持续更新的智能系统。
  • Palantir AIP Bootcamp：先做出结果，再销售长期能力。 Palantir把软件、工程师、行业问题和客户数据放进短期Bootcamp，让客户在几天内用自己的业务场景体验可运行的解决方案，再扩展为长期平台合作——这是"先交付一个真实结果，再出售长期能力"的新获客方式。
  • Deloitte Zora AI：从咨询建议进入持续运营。 为一家消费品公司部署的应付账款处理AI智能体，92%的发票实现无人工接触处理，单张发票处理时间平均减少50%至75%，同时保留人工审核、置信度和审计追踪，是"行业知识＋软件资产＋人工监督＋持续运营"的典型组合。
  • Intercom Fin：按结果收费的邻近样本。 Intercom不是咨询公司，但Fin不按席位或对话数量收费，而是按成功解决的客户问题收费，这一模式在不到一年内创造了数千万美元收入，目前每周解决超过100万次客户服务对话——说明当结果可以定义和验证时，按结果收费不仅可行，还能让供应商和客户的利益真正一致。

这些案例共同说明，所谓成功不是"员工开始使用AI"，而是至少改变了以下一项：交付速度、交付物形态、客户运营方式、收入结构或风险承担方式。

Google Cloud 与 Accenture 已经出现把 forward-deployed AI engineers 直接嵌入客户组织的模式，本质上也是在把"咨询 → 实施 → AI workforce"连起来。

AI 时代的持续高利润技术咨询，本质上是"用 AI 极度压缩交付成本，用领域专长、合规兜底与业务绑定锁定高额总价"：前端依靠极少数具备深厚行业洞察与系统性思维的顶尖架构师锁定高层信任，后端依靠高度 Agent 化的自动化工程体系进行低成本交付。

六、咨询公司的合伙人应如何转型

合伙人首先要主动"蚕食"旧模式。不能一边要求团队提高十倍效率，一边继续用人员规模和计费小时衡量成功。应当重点推动七件事：

  1. 少而深，不多而浅——聚焦少数高价值、可重复、可衡量的行业工作流，而不是泛泛卖"AI战略"。窄而深才能沉淀出真正的行业资产。
  2. 组建小而杂的常设团队——一个业务负责人+一个行业专家+一个AI工程师+一个变革专家+一组Agent，取代大批初级顾问堆人头。
  3. 把项目知识沉淀为公司可复用、可治理的资产——每个项目结束后，问一句"这次学到的东西能不能变成模板/评估集/Agent"；散落在个人电脑和演示文稿里的知识基本等于白做。
  4. 合同结构组合化——固定费用（诊断/原型）+平台/订阅费（持续使用）+托管服务费（运营）+结果分成（可归因场景），单一计费方式已经不够用了。
  5. 建立衡量与治理能力——想收结果费，先要能定义基线、衡量周期、排除外部干扰因素，这本身是一项新能力，不是简单加一条合同条款。
  6. 建立严格的模型评估、人工复核、数据安全和责任边界——AI能力越强，专业责任越不能模糊。
  7. 改造合伙人考核——从签约金额和人员利用率，转向客户结果、经常性收入、资产复用率和客户采用率。

合伙人未来最重要的能力，不再只是销售项目，而是选择值得产品化的问题、设计新的利益分配方式，并让客户愿意共同承担变革。

七、咨询公司的员工应如何转型

员工不应把目标设为"比AI更快地做研究和PPT"，因为这是一场很难获胜的竞争。更有价值的方向，是成为能够指挥、检验和落地AI的专业人员。需要重点培养五类能力：

  • 问题定义：把模糊的管理问题拆成可验证的假设和业务指标；
  • 专业判断：识别模型适用边界、证据缺口和看似合理的错误，知道AI在哪里可信、哪里会出错；
  • AI编排：让模型、数据、工具和人工审核形成稳定工作流；
  • 业务落地：真正理解客户的流程、组织利益和变革阻力；
  • 资产化思维：把每一次分析都当成能否变成模板、数据结构、评价集或智能体来做，而不是一次性交付。

年轻顾问的成长路径也要改变。过去是先做大量基础工作，再逐渐接近客户和决策；未来则应更早进入业务现场，更早理解客户流程，并通过AI承担端到端任务。评价员工的标准，应从"投入了多少时间"转向"独立解决了多复杂的问题，并留下了什么可复用资产"。

简单说：公司要从"卖人力"转向"经营资产"，个人要从"生产答案"转向"设计并监督一个能持续产出答案的系统"。

结语：咨询不会消失，但"出售时间的咨询"会持续贬值

AI并没有消除客户对外部帮助的需求。相反，技术变化越快，企业越需要可信的判断、跨部门协调和实施能力。但客户将越来越不愿为信息搬运、基础分析和大型项目团队本身支付溢价。

未来真正有竞争力的咨询公司，不是拥有最多顾问的公司，而是能够用最小的高级团队、最好的行业资产和最可靠的AI系统，最快形成可验证业务结果的公司。

咨询业过去出售的是"聪明人的时间"；未来出售的将是"由人、数据和智能体共同承担责任的结果"。

所以真正值得建立的不是一个更聪明的咨询团队，而是一个越来越不像咨询公司的咨询公司——从 Consulting，走向 AI Engineering，走向 AI Platform，走向 Managed Service，最终走向 Outcome Sharing。

附录A：未来最危险的咨询业务是什么？

AI 时代，最危险的咨询不是"传统咨询被 AI 替代"，而是"咨询公司的交付效率提高了，但收费逻辑仍然是卖人天"。 具体来说，以下几类业务受冲击最大：

  • Strategy-only："帮 CEO 定义 AI strategy"，AI 会极大压缩这种业务。
  • Generic implementation："帮你部署 Salesforce / ServiceNow / Azure / AWS"。未来 coding agents + vendor professional services 会吃掉大量价值——McKinsey 最新研究甚至发现，约三分之一企业已经因为 agentic coding 能力而选择自己开发原本可能购买的软件。
  • Staff augmentation："给你 20 个 data scientists"，是最不被看好的一类。因为 AI 会让一个人的生产率大幅上升，客户自然会问：为什么还需要 20 个人？
  • PPT consulting：这可能是受 AI 冲击最大的类别，因为 Research → synthesis → slides → recommendation 恰恰是 LLM 最擅长的工作之一。

真正能长期保持高利润的技术咨询，会逐渐从 People → IP → Platform → Outcome → Recurring Revenue，也就是从"卖专家时间"，变成"卖可复制的技术资产 + 持续产生的业务结果"。

附录B：从10M-100M项目到AI原生MVP团队

这类大型项目之所以传统上需要大团队，本质是因为其中大量成本来自"体量"——要读的代码行数、要迁移的数据量、要覆盖的国家/法规数量、要做的测试案例数，这类工作靠人数堆砌，正好是AI压缩得最狠的部分。下面先按具体项目类型拆解，再归纳成一套可复用的模型。

数据迁移与清洗（几十名数据工程师手工做映射）AI辅助字段映射、异常检测、自动生成清洗规则
测试案例编写与执行（大批QA测试员）AI根据流程文档自动生成测试案例，覆盖率往往比人工更全

传统成本构成AI如何压缩
尽调阶段海量财务/HR/IT数据交叉分析AI自动做系统重叠检测、协同效应建模，压缩尽调周期

传统成本构成AI如何压缩

对整合后系统重新做GxP合规验证（大量计算机系统验证CSV文档，大批QA/验证团队）AI根据需求自动生成验证测试脚本和可追溯性矩阵，压缩文档工作量——但人工QA签署本身无法交给AI代劳

MVP团队（6-8人）：首席临床系统架构师（1人）·监管/GxP合规与验证负责人（1人）·临床数据整合AI工程师（2人）·医学编码/数据标准专家（1人）·计算机系统验证（CSV）负责人（1人）·客户关系/项目风险负责人（1人）。

这个领域有一个诚实的特殊限制：GxP验证最终需要一个"责任人"（qualified person）签字担责，这一点AI代替不了。所以和ERP、HR整合不同，这里"稀缺判断"占比的下限不会压得像其他领域那么低——AI能拿掉大部分文档和核对工作量，但拿不掉责任本身。

这七类项目有一个共同结构：传统团队里70%以上的人力，其实是在做"体量工作"（读代码、清数据、写测试、翻译培训材料），只有不到30%是真正稀缺的判断（架构决策、监管解读、变革阻力处理、客户信任）。AI压缩的正是那70%，所以MVP团队的人才构成几乎是固定模式：1名首席架构师、1-2名领域专家、2名AI/工程人才、1名变革/运营负责人、1名客户关系/风险负责人。真正的团队规模天花板，来自能同时并行推进多少个地区/业务单元这类纯带宽问题——如果这才是项目的真正瓶颈，小团队应该老实承认边界，而不是硬接。

为什么小团队反而利润更高

把大团队原来到底在干什么拆成三类工作：

  1. 可复制的认知劳动——读代码、梳理文档缺失的系统、写测试、做数据比对，这类工作靠人数堆砌，因为每个人能处理的信息量有限。
  2. 稀缺的判断——架构决策、风险取舍、客户信任建立，这类工作靠的是少数人的经验，人数堆不出来。
  3. 协调开销——大团队本身需要大量人力去管理大团队，项目经理、对齐会议、交接文档，这部分工作量是团队规模的副产品，不是客户真正需要的价值。

AI压缩的是第1类；第2类原本就是少数人在做，没变；第3类——协调开销——小团队直接消失了，这甚至是净收益，不是要克服的缺点。小团队能顶替大团队的前提是：这个项目里第1类工作占比足够高。如果一个项目的核心难度是"20个工作流需要同时在8个地区并行推进"，那纯粹是并行带宽问题，小团队确实做不到，这种情况应该直接告诉客户不适合，建议找传统大型SI，而不是硬接。

因为第1类工作的成本不再随人头线性增长，而客户拿到的价值（一次成功的迁移、一次完成的转型）没有缩水，利润结构因此同时从三个方向改善：要付的人头更少；同样甚至更高的价格由"确定性"而不是工时来支撑；工具链和方法论playbook可以跨客户复用，持续压低边际交付成本。

如何让中大型企业愿意买单

销售侧，五个动作最关键：

  1. 用低风险试点破冰——先卖一个2-4周的诊断/原型（参考Palantir的Bootcamp打法），让客户在自己的真实数据上看到可运行的结果，再谈大范围合同——买"验证过的确定性"，比买承诺容易得多。
  2. 合伙人个人信誉是核心销售资产——小团队没有品牌背书，只能靠"这个人做过什么"。持续输出行业案例、白皮书、行业会议露面，把个人可信度当成公司的品牌资产来经营。
  3. 绕开传统IT采购、直接对话决策层——系统转型和组织变革本质是战略级问题，应该直接找CIO/CTO/COO建立关系，而不是走标准供应商招标流程——招标流程天然对小团队不利。
  4. 和大型SI/四大做联盟而不是竞争——把自己定位成"大型咨询公司做不动的那20%硬骨头"，通过分包关系借用大公司的采购通道和合规背书，自己只吃技术最深的那一段。
  5. 风险共担条款降低客户决策成本——部分费用与里程碑/结果绑定、失败退款条款、专业责任险——把决策风险从客户身上转移一部分到自己身上，换取更容易的签约。

采购侧——作为客户，你应该要求看到什么，而不是相信承诺：

作为客户，你不应该相信任何"我们能做"的陈述，应该要求以下事项在低风险、可退出的条件下先被证明，再决定是否押上大范围的风险：

  1. 用一个低风险、有退出机制的试点先验证，而不是先签大合同——一个2-4周的诊断/原型阶段，在你的真实系统和真实数据上跑一遍，产出物是可运行的东西（迁移出的一个模块、一份依赖关系图、一次成功的绞杀者迁移试点），不是PPT。这个阶段结束后你有完全的权利说"不行，到此为止"，不需要为后续买单。
  2. 要求看过去项目的实际产出物，不是案例故事——问具体问题："给我看你们上一个类似项目里，AI生成的代码解析报告长什么样""给我看你们的迁移脚本里有多少是人写的、多少是Agent生成后人审核的"。如果对方只能给你讲故事、给不出具体工件，说明方法论没有真正沉淀，只是临场发挥。
  3. 直接打电话给上一个客户的技术负责人，不是看logo墙——不问"你们满意吗"，问"如果这次系统切换出问题，是谁在第一线处理的？响应时间多久？"、"哪个环节让你们最紧张？"——logo墙谁都能贴，但技术负责人的具体细节回忆很难伪造。
  4. 要求AI决策链条可审计——明确问："哪些环节是AI在做决策，哪些环节必须有人复核？如果AI生成的迁移方案出了问题，责任链条是什么？"一个负责任的团队应该能画出这条链路，而不是含糊地说"我们用AI辅助"。
  5. 里程碑分阶段放款，而不是一次性签大合同——每个阶段设可验证的验收标准，验收不通过就停，钱不再往后付。这样下注成本永远只到"下一个里程碑"为止，不是把整个项目的风险一次性押上去。

该警惕的信号：只讲"我们效率高"，不讲"这个项目里哪部分我们做不了"的团队；不愿意做低风险试点，坚持要先签整体大合同的团队；案例只能讲结果（"我们帮XX提升了30%"），讲不出具体技术细节和踩过的坑——很可能是包装过的叙事，不是真实经验。

一句话总结：不应该相信"能做"这句话本身，应该要求它先在小范围、低风险、可退出的条件下变成看得见的东西，再决定要不要把大范围的风险交给对方。任何回避这个验证流程、要求先"信任"再"看证据"的团队，本身就是一个危险信号。

面对复杂系统整合与转型的具体方法论

  • AI辅助系统考古——用代码解析Agent快速摸清遗留系统里的业务逻辑边界、隐藏依赖、数据血缘，把过去几个月的手工梳理压缩到几周。
  • 绞杀者模式（Strangler Fig）迁移——分阶段替换，而不是大爆炸式重写，每个阶段独立可回滚，把风险切成小块。
  • 技术迁移与变革管理并行推进，不是技术做完再处理人的问题——组织接受度往往才是迁移失败的真正原因。
  • 切换后驻留而非"交付即撤"——迁移风险的大头发生在切换后的头六个月，托管运营阶段本身就是交付方法论的一部分，不是附加服务。
